2. The Voyage — Bhupen Hazarika

Summary. Hazarika recalls his journey abroad towards the United States in 1949, travelling through India and Sri Lanka, then taking a sea voyage from Colombo towards Marseille. A forgotten bag containing travel papers briefly delays his departure at Guwahati’s Kanhikuchi Airport. In Sri Lanka he observes cultural links between its dance and Assam’s traditions. On the voyage he notices poverty at Djibouti and memorable sights at sea, including a star near the crescent moon and luminous fish. His outward journey also enlarges his sense of cultural kinship and Indian identity.

Notable details. He planned an indirect route to see more places; the ship journey from Colombo to Marseille was to take thirteen days. The Sri Lankan Yakuma Natum recalls Assam’s deodhani dance. He buys a Rolleiflex camera at Aden; at Djibouti he observes poverty; he does not see the pyramids on this journey. Do not confuse this autobiographical travel account with a generic allegory of a river or attribute it to Katherine Mansfield.

Longer answers to practise

How does travel change Hazarika's sense of home? At first he misses Assam deeply, carrying a drying flower given to him at the airport. Farther from home, he feels attached to all of India. In Sri Lanka, local dance and Buddhist places remind him of Indian and Assamese traditions. He sees that culture can connect people beyond political borders.

What makes the voyage more than a travel diary? Besides places and incidents, Hazarika records how they affect him. The forgotten bag shows a nervous beginning; Sri Lankan dance shows cultural kinship; poverty in Djibouti troubles him; the moon and luminous fish fill him with wonder. The journey becomes an account of curiosity, human sympathy and a widening sense of belonging.
