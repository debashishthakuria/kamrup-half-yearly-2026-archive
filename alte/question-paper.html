Group A · Prose

1 · Answer any five · 1 mark each

a) In which street was Rosemary shopping when she met Miss Smith?

Answer: Rosemary was shopping in Curzon Street.

b) Which Russian author influenced Katherine Mansfield?

Answer: Fyodor Dostoevsky is the Russian author named in the story.The story compares Rosemary’s imagined adventure with a Dostoevsky novel; that is not, by itself, proof of biographical influence on Mansfield.

c) What was the name of the airport situated in Guwahati in 1949?

Answer: It was called Kanhikuchi Airport.

d) How much was the air fare from Guwahati to Dumdum in 1949?

Answer: The fare was 55 rupees.

e) From where did the new vicar hail?

Answer: He came from the East End of London.

f) In the course of how many years did Albert Edward own ten shops?

Answer: In ten years.

g) What is SM Samponio?

Answer: It was the ship on which Hazarika planned to travel from Colombo to Marseille.

2 · Answer any two · 2 marks each

a) Who was Miss Smith? Why did Rosemary send her back?

Answer: Miss Smith was a hungry young woman who asked Rosemary for the price of tea. After Philip called her beautiful, Rosemary grew jealous and sent her away with money.

b) Name the two places associated with Buddhism mentioned by Bhupen Hazarika in 'The Voyage'.

Answer: Sarnath and Bodh Gaya.

c) What did Albert Edward do before he became the verger at St. Peter's?

Answer: He worked in domestic service, beginning as a page-boy and rising through footman to butler in prominent households.

d) Why did Philip disapprove of Rosemary's decision to keep Miss Smith with them?

Answer: Philip thought bringing a stranger home and keeping her there was a mistake. He also remarked that Miss Smith was strikingly pretty, which stirred Rosemary’s jealousy.The text does not clearly say beauty was his stated reason for objecting; do not confuse his remark with his motive.

3 · Answer any two · 3 marks each

a) What happened when the wheels of the plane to Calcutta had started rolling?

Answer: Hazarika realised he had left the bag containing his passport, dollars, tickets and papers behind. He shouted and told the captain, who stopped and brought the plane back. Syed Abdul Malik ran up with the bag, and Hazarika reboarded.

b) Present your views on the food and flavours mentioned by Hazarika in 'The Voyage'?

Answer: Away from home, Hazarika missed familiar Assamese food such as kalmou saak, khar, tenga and bamboo shoot. These tastes recalled his grandmother and home. The details show how a journey can sharpen one’s attachment to local culture.

c) How did the manager react upon his discovery that Mr. Foreman could neither read nor write?

Answer: The bank manager was so surprised that he jumped up from his chair and called it extraordinary. Learning that Foreman was successful despite being unable to read or write, he wondered what Foreman might have become had he been literate.

d) What kind of relationship do Rosemary and Philip share in 'A Cup of Tea'?

Answer: Rosemary and Philip are a wealthy married couple. He speaks to her affectionately, but his praise of Miss Smith’s beauty unsettles her. Her final question, asking whether she is pretty, exposes her need for his reassurance and the insecurity beneath their comfortable life.

4 · Answer any one · 5 marks

a) Describe in detail, how Albert Edward lost the job of the verger at St. Peter's.

Answer: Albert Edward Foreman had served St. Peter’s faithfully for sixteen years. The newly appointed vicar discovered that he could neither read nor write and considered this unacceptable in a modern church. In the presence of two churchwardens, he gave Foreman three months to learn or leave. Foreman said he was too old to learn and would rather resign. Losing the position led him to open a tobacco shop and later build a successful business.

b) Comment on the significance of the title of the story 'A Cup of Tea'.

Answer: Miss Smith asks Rosemary only for the price of a cup of tea because she is hungry and cold. Rosemary instead takes her home, imagining a grand act of charity. Yet her kindness ends when Philip praises the girl’s beauty: she sends Miss Smith away and asks whether she herself is pretty. The simple cup of tea thus begins the encounter that reveals the contrast between genuine need and Rosemary’s vanity. The title keeps the poor woman’s modest request at the heart of the story.

Group B · Poetry

5 · Answer any five · 1 mark each

a) What type of poem is 'Ozymandias of Egypt'?

Answer: It is a sonnet.

b) Who is the poet of the poem 'Strange Meeting'?

Answer: Wilfred Owen.

c) Why does Dickinson describe death as being kind and civil in the poem 'Because I could not stop for Death'?

Answer: She personifies Death as a courteous carriage companion who stops for her and travels without haste.

d) What does the speaker discover in the underworld in the poem 'Strange Meeting'?

Answer: He discovers that the dead soldier he meets is the enemy he killed the day before.

e) Name the collection of poetry in which 'Ozymandias of Egypt' got first published?

Answer: Rosalind and Helen, A Modern Eclogue; with Other Poems (1819), as stated in the Harmony textbook.“First published” is ambiguous: the poem also appeared in a periodical before that collection; this answer follows the textbook’s collection wording.

6 · Answer any two · 2 marks each

a) What are Topper and Tulle in the poem 'Because I could not stop for Death'?

Answer: The poem says “Tippet,” not “Topper.” A tippet is a shoulder covering or shawl; tulle is a thin, fine net fabric. Her light clothing makes the chill more striking.“Topper” appears to be a typo in the supplied paper.

b) What does the poet mean by 'Titanic Wars'?

Answer: Likely the huge, devastating conflicts of war, whose human cost Owen mourns.I could not verify the phrase “Titanic Wars” in the supplied textbook OCR; treat this explanation as tentative, not an official key.

c) What is the only thing remaining in the vast desert?

Answer: The broken statue—its legs, shattered face and pedestal—stands amid the empty, level sands.

d) Which words are used to describe Death in the poem 'Because I could not stop for Death'?

Answer: “Kindly” and “Civility” describe Death’s seemingly courteous manner.

e) Which period of human life is referred to as 'Gazing Grain' in the poem 'Because I could not stop for Death'?

Answer: The ripened grain is commonly read as maturity or adulthood, after the children at school and before the setting sun.This is an interpretation of the image, not an explicit label in the poem.

7 · Answer any one · 5 marks

a) Bring out the central idea contained in the poem 'Ozymandias of Egypt'.

Answer: The ruined statue of Ozymandias stands alone in an empty desert. Its inscription orders the mighty to admire his works, but those works have disappeared. The contrast makes his pride ironic. Time destroys political power, however great a ruler thinks it is. The sculptor’s skill survives in the face carved on the broken stone, while the empire does not. Shelley therefore warns against arrogance and the belief that worldly power can last forever.

b) Write a brief note on Wilfred Owen's representation of the underworld to explore the horrors of war in 'Strange Meeting'.

Answer: The speaker escapes battle through a deep tunnel and meets a dead soldier in a gloomy underworld. The stranger speaks of hopes and truths he can no longer share because war has cut his life short. He then reveals that he is the enemy the speaker killed, yet calls him “my friend.” The meeting strips away the division between opposing armies. In death they recognise their shared humanity, making the suffering and waste of war clearer than any story of military glory.

Group C · Grammar and composition

9 · Add question tags to any five · 1 mark each

a) Nobody came forward to help her, ________?

Answer: did they?

b) Let us go for a walk, ________?

Answer: shall we?

c) She is singing a song, ________?

Answer: isn’t she?

d) Any one can become rich, ________?

Answer: can’t they?“can’t he or she?” may appear in older keys; singular they is natural contemporary usage.

e) Please don't smoke here, ________?

Answer: will you?

f) There are no vehicles on the road, ________?

Answer: are there?

g) We seldom meet now-a-days, ________?

Answer: do we?

10 · Correct any five · 1 mark each

a) One of my friend told me so.

Answer: One of my friends told me so.

b) She is best player in the team.

Answer: She is the best player in the team.

c) He is my cousin brother.

Answer: He is my cousin.

d) They have arrived a week ago.

Answer: They arrived a week ago.

e) Mr. Baruah left India with bag and baggage.

Answer: This sentence is already grammatical: “with bag and baggage” is an idiom meaning with all one’s belongings.The question asks for an error, but this item has no clear error. Avoid inventing a correction.

f) Man is superior than animal.

Answer: Man is superior to animals.A more natural inclusive phrasing is “Humans are superior to animals,” though that comparison is debatable.

11 · Write one paragraph · 5 marks

a) Youth: the Nation builder of tomorrow

Answer: Young people can help shape the nation’s future through education, hard work and service. They bring energy and new ideas to problems in their communities. But enthusiasm alone is not enough: they also need critical thinking, empathy and a willingness to learn from others. A young person who studies sincerely, respects differences and takes part in local work already contributes to society. Schools and families should give youth the skills and opportunities to use their talents responsibly. With guidance and determination, today’s youth can build a fairer and stronger country.

b) Flood in Assam

Answer: Floods are a recurring hardship in Assam, especially for families living near rivers. Heavy rain and overflowing water damage homes, farmland, roads and schools. Many people lose crops or must move to relief camps; animals also suffer. Rescue boats, safe drinking water, medical care and timely warnings are vital during a flood. In the longer term, better planning, strong embankment maintenance where appropriate, protection of wetlands and support for affected families can reduce the damage. Communities must work together before, during and after the floods so that recovery does not leave the most vulnerable behind.

c) Artificial Intelligence

Answer: Artificial intelligence, or AI, is technology that can perform tasks such as recognising patterns, answering questions and helping people organise information. Students can use it to explain a difficult topic, while doctors and researchers may use it to analyse data. However, AI can make mistakes and may reflect bias in its training data. We should check its answers and protect personal information rather than trust it blindly. Used with care, AI can save time and support creativity. Human judgement, honesty and responsibility must remain central to how we use it.

d) Global Warming

Answer: Global warming is the long-term rise in the Earth’s average temperature, driven mainly by greenhouse gases from human activity. It contributes to heat, changing rainfall and other risks to people and nature. Saving electricity, reducing waste, using cleaner energy and protecting forests can help, but action by individuals alone is not enough. Governments and businesses must also cut emissions and prepare communities for changing conditions. Students can begin by learning the science, avoiding waste and encouraging practical changes at home and school. Protecting the climate is a shared responsibility.
