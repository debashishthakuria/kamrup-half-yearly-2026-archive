Section A · Unseen passage

Question 1 · 8 marks (2+2+2+1+1). Read the passage in the supplied PDF, page 2.

1(a)

What do you need to think before you take printouts unnecessarily?

Suggested answerThink of how many trees must be felled to make the paper and whether the printout is really necessary.

1(b)

Name some of the products that can be obtained from recycled paper.

Suggested answerFolders, penholders, teaching-learning materials (TLMs) and paper crafts are examples named in the passage.

1(c)

How does recycled paper conserve natural resources?

Suggested answerIt reduces the need to cut trees and, according to the passage, uses less energy and water than making paper from trees.

1(d)

Give the antonym of conserve.

Suggested answerWaste (or use up).

1(e)

Find a word in the passage which means “the destruction of something, especially waste material by burning”.

Suggested answerIncineration.

Section B · Advanced writing skills

Question 3 · 5 marks. Choose one option in the exam; both models are shown here.

3(main)

Write a factual description of the exhibition-cum-sale programme organised by the school recently.

Suggested answer
Exhibition-cum-Sale at Our School

Our school organised an exhibition-cum-sale in its auditorium recently. The principal inaugurated the event. Students from different classes displayed handmade crafts, paintings, science models and recycled-paper products. Teachers supervised the stalls and explained the exhibits to visitors. Parents and local residents attended and bought many of the student-made items. The proceeds were set aside for the school library. The programme concluded after the principal thanked the participants. It gave students practical experience in teamwork, presentation and handling sales.

Model description: the venue, exhibits and proceeds are illustrative, not verified facts about a real school event.

3(or)

Write a letter to the Editor of “The Assam Tribune” highlighting the rising prices of essential commodities and their impact on common people, with the aim of drawing the attention of the concerned authorities.

Suggested answer
From
A Concerned Citizen
Guwahati, Assam
6 October 2026

To
The Editor
The Assam Tribune
Guwahati

Subject: Rising prices of essential commodities

Sir/Madam,

Through your newspaper, I wish to draw attention to the rising prices of food grains, vegetables, cooking oil and other daily necessities. Families with limited incomes are finding it difficult to meet basic expenses. Students and elderly people are also affected.

I request the authorities to monitor prices, prevent hoarding and improve the supply of essential goods. Publishing regular market-price information would help consumers.

Yours faithfully,
A Concerned Citizen

Section C · Correct verb forms

Question 4 · answer any three of five (3 marks).

4(a)

The boy (sleep), don’t disturb him.

Suggested answerThe boy is sleeping; don’t disturb him.

4(b)

Perhaps it (rain) yesterday.

Suggested answerPerhaps it rained yesterday. (“Perhaps it might have rained yesterday” is possible in a different context, but simple past is the direct fill.)

4(c)

We reached the station after the train (leave).

Suggested answerWe reached the station after the train had left.

4(d)

If I (be) you, I would not do that.

Suggested answerIf I were you, I would not do that.

4(e)

He (work) here since 2009.

Suggested answerHe has been working here since 2009. “Has worked” also works if the employment continues.

Section C · Prepositions

Question 5 · answer any two of five (2 marks).

5(a)

What is the time ____ your watch?

Suggested answerWhat is the time by your watch?

5(b)

She takes pride ____ his wealth.

Suggested answerShe takes pride in his wealth. The pronoun “his” is in the supplied paper.

5(c)

I am very much interested ____ your story.

Suggested answerI am very much interested in your story.

5(d)

Man is superior ____ animal.

Suggested answerMan is superior to animal. More idiomatic: “Human beings are superior to animals”; the original sentence is retained above.

5(e)

The police ran ____ the thief.

Suggested answerThe police ran after the thief.

Section C · Narration

Question 7 · answer any one of two (2 marks).

7(a)

My friend told me that he was very thirsty and requested me to give him a glass of water.

Suggested answerMy friend said to me, “I am very thirsty. Please give me a glass of water.” The exact direct-speech wording may vary while preserving the meaning.

7(b)

One day Mukesh said to me, “I will be a motor mechanic and leave Firozabad.” I said to him, “Do you know anything about car?”

Suggested answerOne day Mukesh told me that he would be a motor mechanic and leave Firozabad. I asked him whether he knew anything about cars. The source says “car” in the direct quote; “cars” in the indirect version is a grammatical adjustment.

Section D · Textbook quick answers

Question 8 · answer any five of seven (5 marks).

8(a)

Why did Gandhi visit Lucknow in 1916?

Suggested answerHe attended the annual convention of the Indian National Congress in Lucknow.

8(b)

Where is Champaran situated?

Suggested answerChamparan is a district in Bihar, near the border with Nepal.

8(c)

Where did Gandhi and Shukla board a train to?

Suggested answerThey travelled by train to Patna. From there Gandhi later travelled towards Muzaffarpur and Champaran.

8(d)

What does the author of “Lost Spring” find Saheb doing every morning?

Suggested answerShe finds Saheb searching for valuable things in garbage dumps.

8(e)

Where was the original home of Saheb’s family?

Suggested answerTheir original home was Dhaka, in Bangladesh.

8(f)

What are mahseers?

Suggested answerMahseers are large freshwater fish, known as game fish.

8(g)

Who is the Chota Sahib in “Memoirs of a Chota Sahib”?

Suggested answerJohn Rowntree, the author and British forest officer.

Section D · Short answers

Question 9 · answer any three of six in 30–40 words (6 marks).

9(a)

“Garbage to them is gold”. What does “garbage” mean to the elders and children, according to Anees Jung?

Suggested answerFor adults, garbage is a means of survival: the scraps they sell provide food and income. For children, it also offers excitement and hope of finding a coin or something valuable among the rubbish.

9(b)

Why was Gandhi impressed with Shukla?

Suggested answerRajkumar Shukla persisted in seeking Gandhi’s help for the Champaran sharecroppers. Although poor and illiterate, he followed Gandhi to different places and waited until Gandhi agreed to visit.

9(c)

What information does the author give us about Manas Wild Life Sanctuary?

Suggested answerRowntree places Manas Sanctuary near Bhutan on the north bank of the Brahmaputra and notes a few rhinoceroses there. He also describes nearby rivers rich in mahseer and the wildlife of the surrounding North Bank. (Flamingo, supplied PDF p. 99.)

9(d)

How was Gandhi able to influence the lawyers? Give instances.

Suggested answerGandhi asked the lawyers what they would do if he were jailed. They initially intended to return home, but, ashamed to abandon the peasants while an outsider risked imprisonment, they pledged to stay and court arrest too.

9(e)

What is the irony inherent in Saheb’s full name?

Suggested answerSaheb-e-Alam means “lord of the universe,” yet Saheb is a poor, barefoot ragpicker with little control over his life. His grand name contrasts sharply with his circumstances.

9(f)

What is Firozabad famous for and why?

Suggested answerFirozabad is famous for its glass-bangle industry. Generations of families, including children in the account, work in its glass furnaces and bangle-making units, often under hazardous conditions.

(i) Extract from “Keeping Quiet” (see the supplied PDF for the poem).

10i(a)

How long does the poet want to stay still?

Suggested answerFor a brief moment—while counting to twelve.

Why does he ask us to keep still and not use any language?

What does the poet mean by “not move our arms so much”?

Section D · Poetry short answers

Question 11 · answer any two of four (4 marks).

11(a)

What is the “sadness” that the poet refers to in “Keeping Quiet”?

Suggested answerIt is the sadness of never understanding ourselves because we are always busy and caught up in destructive pursuits. The poet hopes a moment of stillness will help us reflect. (Flamingo, supplied PDF p. 113.)

11(b)

What is the “childish longing” that the poet refers to? Why is it “vain”?

Suggested answerThe roadside-stand owners long for passing motorists to stop and buy something. Their hope is vain because most travellers ignore them or stop only to ask directions or for other reasons.

11(c)

How did the travellers on the highways react to the roadside stand?

Suggested answerThey mostly drove past without stopping. Some complained that the stand spoiled the scenery; those who did stop often asked for directions or fuel rather than buying its produce.

11(d)

What childhood fear did Kamala Das refer to in “My Mother at Sixty-Six”? How did she hide it?

Suggested answerShe feared losing her mother to death or separation. At the airport she concealed her anxiety by smiling and repeatedly telling her mother, “See you soon.”

Section D · The Tiger King

Question 12 · answer any two of three (4 marks).

12(a)

What did the astrologer predict about the Tiger King?

Suggested answerThe astrologers predicted that the prince would die because of a tiger. Later the warning centred on the hundredth tiger.

12(b)

Who is the Tiger King? Why did he get that name?

Suggested answerHe is the Maharaja of Pratibandapuram, Jilani Jung Jung Bahadur. The story calls him the Tiger King because his fate is bound to tigers; he obsessively hunts them to evade the prediction of his death.

12(c)

How did the Tiger King celebrate the killing of the hundredth tiger?

Suggested answerBelieving he had killed it, he ordered a grand procession to the capital. The tiger was later buried and a tomb raised. In fact his shot missed; the hunters killed it afterwards.
