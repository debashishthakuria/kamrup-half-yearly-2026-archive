Flamingo · Anees Jung

Lost Spring

Saheb-e-Alam searches Seemapuri rubbish for survival; Mukesh of Firozabad hopes to break from his family’s bangle-making work and become a motor mechanic.

Textbook: Flamingo, supplied textbook PDF pp. 22–30 (page numbers counted from first PDF page) · Original practice, not printed paper questions.

Check your recall

Where did Saheb’s family come from, and why?
Suggested answer

They came from Dhaka in Bangladesh; storms had swept away their home and fields.

Why is Saheb’s name ironic?
Suggested answer

Saheb-e-Alam means “lord of the universe”, but he is a poor child who searches garbage for a living.

What changes when Saheb takes a tea-stall job?
Suggested answer

He earns 800 rupees and meals, but loses the freedom he had as a ragpicker; the canister belongs to the owner.

What does Mukesh want to become?
Suggested answer

A motor mechanic. His ambition contrasts with his family’s inherited bangle-making trade.

What does garbage mean to adults and children?
Suggested answer

To adults it is survival; to children it can also hold wonder and the hope of finding a coin.

Flamingo · Louis Fischer

Indigo

Rajkumar Shukla brings Gandhi to Champaran, where indigo sharecroppers face unjust conditions. Legal resistance, an inquiry and local social work turn fear into collective action.

Textbook: Flamingo, supplied textbook PDF pp. 55–64 (page numbers counted from first PDF page) · Original practice, not printed paper questions.

Check your recall

Why was Gandhi at Lucknow in 1916?
Suggested answer

For the Indian National Congress convention, where Shukla approached him.

How did Shukla convince Gandhi to go to Champaran?
Suggested answer

He followed Gandhi persistently and waited for him at their agreed meeting in Calcutta.

Where did Gandhi and Shukla travel together by train?
Suggested answer

To Patna, before Gandhi went towards Muzaffarpur and Champaran.

Why did the lawyers agree to stay?
Suggested answer

Gandhi asked what would happen to the peasants if he went to jail. They felt it would be shameful to abandon the peasants while an outsider risked imprisonment.

Why did Gandhi accept only a partial refund?
Suggested answer

The amount mattered less than making the landlords surrender some power and restoring the peasants’ confidence.

Flamingo · John Rowntree

Memoirs of a Chota Sahib

A forest officer recalls the Brahmaputra, Peacock Island, contrasting north and south banks, monsoon journeys and forest bungalows around pre-Independence Guwahati.

Textbook: Flamingo, supplied textbook PDF pp. 98–104 (page numbers counted from first PDF page) · Original practice, not printed paper questions.

Check your recall

Who is the Chota Sahib?
Suggested answer

John Rowntree, the author and former British Senior Conservator of Forests of Assam.

What was visible from his bungalow veranda?
Suggested answer

The Brahmaputra and river traffic, the Himalayas beyond it, and Peacock Island in the foreground.

What does the account say about Manas?
Suggested answer

Manas Sanctuary bordered Bhutan and had a few rhinoceroses. Nearby rivers held mahseer, a freshwater fish.

What was a mar boat?
Suggested answer

A river ferry: a plank platform over two side-by-side boats, paddled or driven by the current along a cable.

Why was Kulsi the author’s favourite bungalow?
Suggested answer

It was pleasantly situated above the river and surrounded by teak plantations, with a rubber plantation nearby.

Flamingo · A. R. Barton

Going Places

Sophie fantasises about glamorous work and meeting footballer Danny Casey. Her friend Jansie sees the family’s economic limits, while Geoff becomes a confidant for her imagined world.

Textbook: Flamingo, supplied textbook PDF pp. 86–96 (page numbers counted from first PDF page) · Original practice, not printed paper questions.

Check your recall

What work did Jansie expect for herself and Sophie?
Suggested answer

Jobs at the biscuit factory after school.

What careers did Sophie imagine?
Suggested answer

Running a boutique, managing a shop, acting and fashion design.

Why does Sophie confide in Geoff?
Suggested answer

His quiet, seemingly wider world fascinates her; she hopes to share the world beyond their neighbourhood.

Did Danny Casey meet Sophie by the canal?
Suggested answer

No. She waits for him, but nobody comes; the story frames their supposed earlier encounter as her fantasy.

What does the contrast between Sophie and Jansie show?
Suggested answer

Sophie is an imaginative dreamer; Jansie is practical about money and their likely jobs.

Flamingo · Kamala Das

My Mother at Sixty-Six

A drive to Cochin airport makes the speaker confront her ageing mother and her old fear of separation. Energetic trees and children contrast with the mother’s frailty.

Textbook: Flamingo, supplied textbook PDF pp. 107–108 (page numbers counted from first PDF page) · Original practice, not printed paper questions.

Check your recall

What does the speaker notice about her mother in the car?
Suggested answer

She is dozing with her mouth open; her face appears pale and ashen.

Why are the young trees and merry children significant?
Suggested answer

They evoke movement and youth, contrasting with her mother’s age and stillness.

What is the childhood fear?
Suggested answer

Fear of losing her mother and being separated from her.

Why does the speaker smile at parting?
Suggested answer

She masks her anxiety and tries to reassure herself and her mother with a hopeful farewell.

Flamingo · Pablo Neruda

Keeping Quiet

Neruda proposes a brief pause in speech and action for shared reflection, non-violence and self-understanding; he explicitly rejects death and permanent inactivity.

Textbook: Flamingo, supplied textbook PDF pp. 112–114 (page numbers counted from first PDF page) · Original practice, not printed paper questions.

Check your recall

Why does he ask everyone to count to twelve?
Suggested answer

To create a brief shared pause for silence and reflection.

Does the poet advocate permanent inactivity?
Suggested answer

No. He distinguishes his momentary stillness from death and total inactivity.

What is the sadness in the poem?
Suggested answer

People fail to understand themselves and threaten themselves through destructive actions.

What does the Earth teach?
Suggested answer

Apparent stillness can conceal renewal and continuing life.

Flamingo · Robert Frost

A Roadside Stand

A roadside seller hopes passing city traffic will buy farm produce. Instead, motorists ignore the stand, object to its signs or stop for other reasons; the poem criticises unequal access to prosperity.

Textbook: Flamingo, supplied textbook PDF pp. 117–119 (page numbers counted from first PDF page) · Original practice, not printed paper questions.

Check your recall

What do the sellers hope to obtain?
Suggested answer

Some city money from customers buying their produce, not charity.

What is their childish longing?
Suggested answer

To hear a vehicle stop and have a traveller ask the price of their goods; it is usually disappointed.

How do motorists react?
Suggested answer

Most pass by. Some dislike the signs, and those who stop may ask for directions, fuel or room to turn around instead of buying.

What does the poet criticise in the promised help?
Suggested answer

Powerful benefactors claim to help the rural poor but take away independence and do not meet their real needs.

Vistas · Kalki

The Tiger King

A ruler hunts tigers to evade a prediction that a tiger will cause his death. The hundredth animal survives his shot; later an infected splinter from a wooden toy tiger kills him.

Textbook: Vistas, supplied textbook PDF pp. 9–14 (page numbers counted from first PDF page) · Original practice, not printed paper questions.

Check your recall

What prediction was made at his birth?
Suggested answer

He would die because of a tiger; later the astrologer warned especially of the hundredth tiger.

How many tigers had he killed before searching for the last one?
Suggested answer

Ninety-nine. The hundredth survived his shot and was killed by hunters afterwards.

Why did he marry a princess from another state?
Suggested answer

Her state had many tigers, giving him more opportunities to hunt after those in his own state ran out.

How did he die?
Suggested answer

A splinter from a wooden toy tiger pierced his hand, became infected, and the king died after an operation.

Vistas · Zitkala-Sa and Bama

Memories of Childhood

Two autobiographical accounts show children confronting oppression: Zitkala-Sa resists the forced cutting of her hair at school; Bama learns why an elder must carry food without touching it and resolves to study hard.

Textbook: Vistas, supplied textbook PDF pp. 52–55 (page numbers counted from first PDF page) · Original practice, not printed paper questions.

Check your recall

Why does Zitkala-Sa resist the haircut?
Suggested answer

In her culture short or shingled hair carried shame; she resists having it forced upon her.

Who warned her about the haircut?
Suggested answer

Her friend Judewin, who understood a little English.

Why did the elder hold the food packet by its string?
Suggested answer

Caste prejudice forbade him from touching the food intended for the landlord.

What advice does Bama’s brother give her?
Suggested answer

Study diligently and progress so that others cannot so easily deny her dignity.

What unites the two accounts?
Suggested answer

Both show children recognising and resisting different forms of discrimination.

Grammar · original practice

Tense

Choose a verb form using the time marker and sequence of events; a continuing state from a past point may take the present perfect or present perfect continuous.

By the time the bus arrived, we ___ (wait) for an hour.
Suggested answer

had been waiting

An ongoing past action preceded another past event.
The girl ___ (read) now; please keep quiet.
Suggested answer

is reading

“Now” points to an action in progress.
I ___ (know) my friend since 2017.
Suggested answer

have known

A state begun in the past and continuing now; “know” is normally not progressive.
If I ___ (be) you, I would revise the poems.
Suggested answer

were

Hypothetical condition: “If I were you”.

Grammar · original practice

Voice

Keep tense and meaning; make the active object the passive subject. Retain necessary prepositions such as “at”.

The committee has announced the winners. (Passive)
Suggested answer

The winners have been announced by the committee.

Present perfect passive: have/has been + past participle.
They laughed at the speaker. (Passive)
Suggested answer

The speaker was laughed at by them.

Keep the preposition “at”.
The letter was written by Rima. (Active)
Suggested answer

Rima wrote the letter.

Past passive becomes simple past active.
Close the gate. (Passive)
Suggested answer

Let the gate be closed.

Passive form of an imperative.

Grammar · original practice

Narration

Reported statements and questions change pronouns and, when a past reporting verb requires it, often backshift tense. Commands use “told/asked + object + to”.

Rina said, “I am preparing a poster.” (Indirect)
Suggested answer

Rina said that she was preparing a poster.

Past reporting verb; am preparing → was preparing.
He said to me, “Where do you live?” (Indirect)
Suggested answer

He asked me where I lived.

Use statement word order in the reported question.
The teacher said, “Do not waste paper.” (Indirect)
Suggested answer

The teacher told us not to waste paper.

Negative instruction: told + object + not to.
She said that she had finished her work. (Direct)
Suggested answer

She said, “I have finished my work.”

One valid reconstruction; exact original wording cannot be recovered.

Grammar · original practice

Preposition

Learn phrases in context: interested in, pride in, superior to, responsible for, insist on.

He takes pride ___ his work.
Suggested answer

in

“Take pride in” is the fixed phrase.
She is interested ___ history.
Suggested answer

in

“Interested in” is the fixed phrase.
The team insisted ___ an explanation.
Suggested answer

on

“Insist on” takes on.
We are responsible ___ the library books.
Suggested answer

for

“Responsible for” is the fixed phrase.
This route is superior ___ the older one.
Suggested answer

to

“Superior to,” not “superior than”.

Grammar · original practice

Transformation of sentences

Change the structure without changing meaning; more than one answer can be valid.

He was too tired to continue. (Use “so … that”)
Suggested answer

He was so tired that he could not continue.

Too … to corresponds to so … that + cannot/could not.
Although it rained, they played. (Use “despite”)
Suggested answer

Despite the rain, they played.

Despite needs a noun phrase or -ing form.
As soon as she arrived, the programme began. (Use “no sooner”)
Suggested answer

No sooner had she arrived than the programme began.

No sooner + had + subject + past participle + than.
Only Rima can solve this problem. (Begin “No one”)
Suggested answer

No one except Rima can solve this problem.

Exclusive meaning preserved.

Writing practice

The paper offered notice or advertisement, and factual description or a letter. Poster is in your syllabus but did not appear as a direct choice in the supplied paper. These models use invented institutions, dates and people; change them to fit the task.

Writing · original model

Notice

Give the institution/issuing authority, NOTICE, date, specific heading, essential what/when/where/how and name/designation. Follow the question’s word limit.

Practice prompt

Your literary club is collecting student poems for its annual magazine. Draft a notice in about 50 words.

Reveal one possible model
HILL VIEW SCHOOL · NOTICE
12 August 2026
Poems for the School Magazine

Original poems by students are invited for the annual magazine. Submit a typed copy with your name and class to the library desk by 22 August. Selected entries will be edited for publication. For queries, contact the undersigned.

Rina Das
Literary Club Secretary

Writing · original model

Poster

Use a large headline, a memorable call to action, two or three actionable points and an issuer/contact. Keep copy short and readable.

Practice prompt

Make a poster encouraging students to save paper.

Reveal one possible model
SAVE PAPER. KEEP TREES STANDING.

Print only what you need · Use both sides · Recycle clean paper

Bring old notebooks to the collection box outside the library every Friday.

Green Club, Hill View School

Writing · original model

Advertisement

For a classified ad, use a category headline, concise description, relevant location, contact and any necessary identification. Do not publish sensitive document numbers.

Practice prompt

You have lost a blue folder between Guwahati and Nalbari. Draft a classified ad in under 50 words.

Reveal one possible model
LOST DOCUMENTS

Blue file folder lost while travelling from Guwahati to Nalbari on 10 August 2026. Contains academic certificates and notes. Finder may contact Rina at 9XXXXXXXXX. Reward offered. Please do not misuse the documents.

Writing · original model

Letter to the editor

Use sender address, date, recipient, subject, salutation, a factual problem and a clear request, followed by a suitable closing and signature.

Practice prompt

Write to the editor about unsafe pedestrian crossings near schools.

Reveal one possible model
12, Station Road
Guwahati, Assam
12 August 2026

The Editor
The Assam Tribune
Guwahati

Subject: Safer crossings near schools

Sir/Madam,

Through your newspaper, I wish to draw attention to unsafe crossings near schools. Heavy traffic during arrival and dispersal puts children at risk, especially where signs and pedestrian markings are missing.

I request the traffic authorities to mark crossings clearly, enforce speed limits and arrange safe crossing support at peak hours.

Yours faithfully,
Rina Das
